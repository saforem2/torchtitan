## Why it ended

Two unrelated causes, neither numerical:

**Trainer 0 (04:27)** -- clean `FAILOVER STOP: walltime (rc=143)` after 17,971s.
Hit its own budget, saved, exited. Working as designed.

**Trainer 1 (05:50)** -- Aurora infrastructure:

```
ping failed on x4713c6s2b0n0: No reply from x4410c7s1b0n0 after 97s
[auto-retry] blind rotation: x4713c5s2b0n0...
launch failed: Couldn't forward RPC launch(...) : Resource temporarily unavailable
[auto-retry] FAILOVER STOP: stuck_pre_training (rc=127)
```

A node went unresponsive, auto-retry correctly rotated it out, and the
**relaunch** hit the known Aurora pals RPC fault. Failover gave up. The umbrella
then SIGTERM'd trainers 2/3/4 at the same second (`05:50:21`), which is why
trainer 2 stops mid-stride at step 7897 with nothing wrong with it.

So the tally is: **1 clean walltime exit, 1 infra fault, 3 collateral kills** --
not five failures.

## Three things worth following up

**1. 20B-512 loss ROSE across the run: 2.18 -> 2.41.** Not noise, and not a
spike -- it climbs monotonically over ~350 steps:

```
step 6801  2.2336      step 7009  2.4069
step 6871  2.2771      step 7113  2.4227
step 6905  2.3651      step 7148  2.4150
```

The chain resumed from step-6800 and immediately drifted upward. Both 20B chains
run `decay-ratio=0.0` (constant LR 2.28e-5), so this is not a schedule artifact.
Compare trainer 2 on the same config, which stayed flat (2.3765 -> 2.3710) over
400 steps. **A resume that comes back 0.23 nats worse and keeps climbing is worth
explaining before the next 512N dispatch** -- candidates: optimizer state not
restored cleanly across the resume, a data-order/shard difference, or genuine
instability at this LR for the 512N batch. Note the 256N chain now reports a
LOWER loss (2.371) than the 512N chain (2.415) despite having seen roughly half
the tokens.

**2. 2B-512 MFU is ~3-9%, against 17-20% for the 20B trainers.** Ramps 0.46% ->
~8% and oscillates 5-9% for the rest of the run, ending at 2.70%. tflops 8.04
versus 52-59 for the 20B chains. That is a large amount of wasted 512-node
allocation and has been true for a while (this is the same chain that has been
"queue-starved" in the dashboard). Worth a separate look at whether it is
comms-bound at this size.

**3. BOTH constant-LR forks failed to load their seed checkpoint, and the
failure was mislabelled as a walltime stop.** Trainers 3 and 4 are not spare or
duplicate slots -- they are the two constant-LR experiments:

| trainer | ckpt dir | LR | decay-ratio |
|---------|----------|----|-------------|
| 3 | `...n512-gbs12288-constlr-from9200` | 2.28e-5 | 0.0 |
| 4 | `...n256-gbs6144-constlr-from9500` | 2.28e-5 | 0.0 |

Both died in the same place:

```
trainer.py:65  self.checkpointer.load(step=config.checkpoint.load_step)
torch.distributed.checkpoint.api.CheckpointException
```

They never reached step 1 -- this is a checkpoint-load failure during setup, not
a training problem.

**Root cause for trainer 3 (FIXED 2026-08-06).** The error is explicit:

```
FileNotFoundError: --checkpoint.load_step=9260 but checkpoint
  .../constlr-from9200/step-9260 [not found]
```

Nothing sets `load_step` in the umbrella spec -- `trainer.py:869` passes
`config.checkpoint.load_step` straight through, so 9260 came from resolving
"latest". The fork dir held three entries:

```
step-9200                    6144 entries  <- the intended fork point, complete
step-9250-20260729-143406      24 entries  <- aborted partial save
step-9260-20260729-143406      24 entries  <- aborted partial save
```

The 07-29 cleanup backup-RENAMED the fragments in place, which is not enough: a
`step-*` scan still sees them, resolves latest to 9260, and then fails because
the *unsuffixed* `step-9260` does not exist. Fixed by moving both fragments out
of the checkpoint tree to `_aborted-fragments/n512-constlr-from9200/` (with a
README recording the rule: a partial save must be moved OUT, not renamed
inside). `step-9200` is now the only entry, so latest resolves correctly.

**Trainer 4's cause is NOT yet established.** Its checkpoint dir is clean --
`step-9500` with all 3072 shards and `.metadata`, no fragments -- so the
stale-latest explanation does not apply to it. It raises the same
`CheckpointException` from the same `checkpointer.load()` line, but the
per-rank detail is truncated in the console log and the underlying reason has
not been read out. Do not assume it is the same bug. Yet both report `FAILOVER STOP: walltime (rc=143,
attempt 1)` after 618s and 1109s. A walltime stop 10 minutes into a 24h job is
plainly wrong, and the mislabel is what made these look ignorable: the summary
banner shows five identical-looking failures, so two dead experiments hid behind
three collateral SIGTERMs.

This is task #38's experiment (2B-512 constant-LR fork from step-9200) plus a
sibling from step-9500. The umbrella was their delivery vehicle and they got no
compute out of it. Trainer 3 is fixed; **trainer 4 still needs diagnosis before the next
umbrella dispatch, or that slot burns its share of ~2k nodes again.**
