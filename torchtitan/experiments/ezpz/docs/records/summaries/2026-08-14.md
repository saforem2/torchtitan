## 2. Sunspot: reducing collectives were broken; the RC fixes them

Several 80B runs across this window failed for one shared reason, traced to
the stack rather than to our code.

**Symptom.** `agpt_80b` at 4N/TP=4 -- a config that ran 10/10 clean on 08-03
(job `12472452`) -- SIGSEGV'd on every rank inside step 1, in
`_redistribute_outputs` -> `reduce_scatter_tensor`.

**It was not our code.** An A/B at the 08-03 commit and at HEAD failed
*identically* (job `12473110`, 60 segv each, 0 steps). A ~40-line torch-only
script -- no torchtitan, no DTensor, no model -- segfaults on a **1 KiB buffer
with 12 ranks on a single node**, so no fabric is involved either. Reproduced in
6 allocations with a different failing rank each time (34, 12, 33, 18, 8, 4), so
not a bad node.

**It is the reduction specifically.** Same node, same ranks, same buffers:

| collective | result |
|---|---|
| `all_gather_into_tensor` | **passes** all sizes to 144 MiB/rank |
| `reduce_scatter_tensor` | SIGSEGV |
| `all_reduce` | SIGSEGV |

Both failures reduce on device; the one that passes only moves bytes. Since
`all_reduce` is DDP's and FSDP's gradient reduction, this blocked **all**
multi-rank training, not just TP>1.

**The frameworks RC fixes it.** On `frameworks/2026.1.0` (oneCCL `26.181.0` +
torch `2.13.0a0+gitcf30153`) all three collectives run clean. No workaround
exists among ten standard CCL knobs (transport, IPC exchange, immediate
commandlists, monolithic kernel, worker count).

Docs: [sunspot-reduce-scatter-segv-20260814](../../reference/known-bugs/sunspot-reduce-scatter-segv-20260814.md)
(includes a verified one-node repro + a drafted ALCF ticket) and
[`tests/repro_reduce_scatter_segv.py`](../../tests/repro_reduce_scatter_segv.py).

## 4. The RC does NOT fix the 80B NaN -- and fp32 shows why

bf16 at dp=192 on the RC **NaN'd at step 30** (job `12473142`), landing in the
historical 14/17/19/38 ladder. **Wall 1 is stack-independent.**

Running the same config with fp32 activations (job `12473149`) exposed the
mechanism:

| step | bf16 grad_norm | fp32 grad_norm | bf16 loss | fp32 loss |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 8.33 | **17,418** | 15.46 | 12.84 |
| 7 | 7.86 | **91,467** | 12.90 | 12.75 |
| 11 | 8.05 | **80,065** | 12.76 | **12.39** |

**bf16 is not mis-reporting a large gradient -- it computes a different, smaller
one.** bf16 has fp32's full exponent range (max ~3.4e38), so 18,000 is trivially
representable; 65504 is the *fp16* limit. What bf16 loses is its **7-bit
mantissa**, destroying the small contributions whose accumulation across 84
residual layers produces those spikes. Mantissa, not exponent -- exactly what
the root-cause report said.

Corollaries:

- `grad_norm ~= 8` in every historical 80B bf16 log is a real value for a
  *degraded* gradient, not a masked reading of the true one.
- **Clipping is not broken.** It scales by `1/total_norm` using the norm computed
  in that run, and the bf16 run genuinely has norm ~8.
- **fp32 learns faster** (12.39 vs 12.76 at step 11, accelerating), which a pure
  reporting artifact could not produce.

Measured cost at dp=192: memory 36.5 GiB (57%) vs 20.5 (32%); **16 tps vs 54 =
3.4x slower**, matching the documented 3-5x.

At the time of writing the fp32 run is at step 23, clean, past the 14/17/19
points. Step 30 -- where bf16 died at this dp -- is the open question.

### 9.4a What happened next: the model itself got built and measured

9.4 tested the proposal's *claims* on paper. The day after, the model was
built and run. Full write-ups, all under
[`docs/records/proposals/30b-exp/`](../production/agpt/30b-exp/):

| doc | what it settles |
|---|---|
| [exp05 -- 30B at 2N](../proposals/30b-exp/exp05-2n-performance.md) | it trains; TP hurts; AC is mandatory; batch is the lever; HSDP fails |
| [exp06 -- weak scaling 2N to 64N](../proposals/30b-exp/exp06-scaling.md) | holds ~26% MFU to 64N; 128N beats 512N by 1.53x per GPU |
| [exp07 -- tokenizer bake-off](../proposals/30b-exp/exp07-custom-tokenizer-feasibility.md) | a custom 64k loses; OLMo-2 wins; confirmed on hardware |

Detail lives in [section 10](#10-addendum-2026-08-16--the-30b-goes-from-proposal-to-measured-model).
The short version, and how it lands against 9.4's table:

**The proposal's central claim is CONFIRMED in mechanism, roughly half in
magnitude.** It argued that recovering ~27% MFU from the 2B's 8.79% at 512N
is a "~3x effective-compute multiplier". The 30B does reach **28.9% MFU at
2N** and holds **25.5% at 64N**, decaying 1.42% per doubling against the 2B's
13.96% -- so bigger really does use the hardware better. But at 512N a sane
global batch forces LBS=1, which measures **~17%**, i.e. **1.95x** the 2B,
not 3x. The ~27% figure is real and lives at **128N**.

**"Shrink the vocab" -- 9.4's best-surviving claim -- survives, but not the
way it was argued.** The proposal wanted a custom ~64k BPE. Measured on 176 MB
of held-out corpus text against nine tokenizers, a 64k trained on our own data
is **2.5% WORSE than gemma** and 5.9% worse than Llama-3: 705 MB cannot
compete with tokenizers fit on hundreds of GB, and dclm (94.8% of the corpus)
is generic web text that Llama-3 already covers. Fertility also **saturates
above ~100k** -- 128k->100k costs 0.09%, 100k->64k costs 6.2% -- so ~64k sits
just past the knee. The win is **OLMo-2's 100,352**, which ties the best on
fertility with a 22% smaller vocab, and on hardware delivers Llama-3's
throughput with **~9 points more HBM headroom**.

**"Embedding cost ~26%" is corrected again.** 9.4 revised it up to 52.8% at
2B. At the 30B's dim=6144 the gemma embedding is **11.2%** of parameters, and
the OLMo-2 vocab cuts it to 3.0%. More usefully, the *memory* cost is
dominated by the **logits tensor** (`batch x seq x vocab`), not the parameters
-- FSDP shards params across 24 ranks, so their contribution is ~0.2 GB, while
logits are per-rank and scale with batch.

**New findings 9.4 could not have had:**

- **HSDP works on the 20B and fails on the 30B**, at 71.68% memory with 18 GiB
  free -- level_zero *resource* exhaustion in gradient clipping, not an OOM.
  Contradicts exp03's "+2.7% MFU, prefer HSDP".
- **Noise has two scales:** <1% within a job, **~3% across jobs**. Any
  cross-job difference under ~3% is not evidence. Memory, by contrast,
  reproduces byte-for-byte.
- **The freed memory is worth having as margin, not batch.** LBS 5->7 buys
  +1.8% tps for +18 points of HBM.

**Still open:** 512N needs Aurora; a GBS sanity bound matters more than MFU
for choosing node count; and the newer-data question (survey: keep the DCLM
bulk, replace the 79B specialist tail with PDF-sourced text) is unresolved.

### 9.4b The embedding was frozen too -- and nobody had checked

[exp02](../proposals/30b-exp/exp02-fp32-norms-ablation.md), three arms,
identical seed/data/steps, differing only in the master copy (job `8757151`):

| arm | norms frozen | `tok_embeddings` frac_changed |
|---|---|---|
| A bf16 | **13/13** | 0.000346 |
| B **norms-only fp32** | 0/13 | **0.000345** |
| C full fp32 | 0/13 | **1.000000** |

**38.36% of all parameter elements never move under norms-only.** Cause: agpt
initialises the embedding at `std=1.0` (`_EMBEDDING_INIT`) -- the *same scale
as `RMSNorm.weight`*, hence the same ~7.8e-3 bf16 ULP. The v1 post-mortem
reasoned only about linear layers at std~0.005 and never checked the embedding.
**The bug had already recurred in the shipped 2B/20B/80B configs.**

Coverage ruled out decisively (job `8757243`): restricted to the 91/32,000 rows
that actually received gradient -- identical rows in all arms -- full fp32
moves 1.0000 while norms-only moves 0.1471, indistinguishable from the broken
arm. QK-norm confirmed too: bf16 freezes **25/25** norms with it enabled.

And full fp32 is **also the fastest** (54.0 vs 39.0 vs 29.8 tps at 24 ranks) --
FSDP2 forbids mixed dtypes per shard group, so norms-only needs its own
collectives. It loses on correctness, throughput, and complexity at once.

**Loss cannot see any of this**: all three arms within 0.005 nats, and the
*broken* arm has the lowest final loss.

48H, 8kv, ffn 16384, head_dim 128 matching both neighbours -- 28.1B params.
[exp05](../proposals/30b-exp/exp05-2n-performance.md):

| config | tps/GPU | MFU | memory |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| uncompiled, LBS=1 | 340 | 20.35% | 73.0% |
| compiled, LBS=1 | 360 | 21.54% | 68.1% |
| compiled, LBS=2 | 434 | 25.99% | 80.6% |
| **compiled, LBS=3** | **466** | **27.89%** | **78.5%** |

- **TP hurts monotonically** -- TP=2 costs 26%, TP=4 costs 55%. The model fits
  at 2N without it, so there is no memory reason to pay. Same pattern the 80B
  shows: TP earns its keep only when the model does not otherwise fit.
- **LBS=3 is faster AND cheaper in memory than LBS=2** (78.5% vs 80.6%).
  Larger batches amortize the activation peak, so "bigger batch costs more
  memory" inverts across that step.
- **Activation checkpointing is load-bearing.** Both `ac=none` and
  `ac=selective` are out-of-memory at LBS=2: `none` reports it directly
  (746 MiB requested, 126 MiB free), `selective` surfaces it inside FSDP's
  `post_backward` -> `foreach_reduce` -> `reduce_scatter_single` as
  `RuntimeError: Enqueue process failed` / `level_zero error 39
  (UR_RESULT_ERROR_OUT_OF_DEVICE_MEMORY)`. The generic message made this look
  like a third, separate failure; it is not.

  Worth keeping the two level_zero codes apart: **39 is out-of-device-MEMORY**
  (here), **40 is out-of-RESOURCES** (the HSDP failure in 10.4). Different
  codes, different causes -- which is independent support for 10.4's
  conclusion that HSDP is not an OOM.

This directly updates 9.4's stale-MFU row: the 30B reaches **27.89%** at small
N against the 2B's 29.26%, on a model 17x larger.

### 10.2 Scaling: 1.42% decay per doubling vs the 2B's 13.96%

[exp06](../proposals/30b-exp/exp06-scaling.md), job `12473198`, one job so
all six points share a stack, build and allocation:

| nodes | ranks | tps/GPU | MFU | vs 2N |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 24 | 458 | 27.45% | 100% |
| 4 | 48 | 451 | 27.03% | 98.5% |
| 8 | 96 | 443 | 26.53% | 96.7% |
| 16 | 192 | 445 | 26.67% | 97.2% |
| 32 | 384 | 440 | 26.36% | 96.1% |
| 64 | 768 | 426 | 25.54% | 93.0% |

**This is the first direct evidence for the proposal's central claim** -- the
one thing 9.4 could not test. Flat within 1.9 points across a 32x scale-up.

Two caveats kept in the doc rather than buried:

- **64N is three doublings short of 512N**, and the 2B's collapse was a cliff,
  not a slope -- extrapolating through smooth terrain cannot locate a cliff.
- **At 64N the 2B itself still runs 25.9%**, nearly identical to the 30B's
  25.54%. The measured range does not yet *separate* the two models; it shows
  the 30B does not degrade earlier. Necessary, not sufficient.

**The constraint the proposal omits -- stated correctly.** My first version of
this said global batch *necessarily* grows with node count, so LBS=3 at 512N
would force GBS 18,432 / 75M tokens per step, past the ceiling the 2B already
measured (GBS 12,288 lost 3-8pp on lm-eval per token against 6,144, with
per-step parity confirming the architecture was fine).

**That framing was wrong.** `global_batch_size` is a config field
(`configs.py:35`), and gradient accumulation is *derived* from it
(`trainer.py:425`): `GAS = GBS / (LBS * dp_degree)`. It only *looks* like GBS
tracks node count because the default is `-1`, which falls back to
`LBS * dp_degree` (`trainer.py:410-414`). Pin `--training.global-batch-size`
and GBS is fixed at any scale.

So the real constraint is tighter and different. With GBS pinned at the 2B's
known-good 6,144, GAS absorbs the node count only while `LBS * dp <= GBS`:

| nodes | ranks | max LBS at GBS 6,144 | GAS |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 64 | 768 | 8 | 2 at LBS=4 |
| **128** | 1536 | **4** | 1 |
| 256 | 3072 | 2 | 1 |
| 512 | 6144 | **1** | 1 |

**128N is where the best config runs out of room.** Past it, holding a sane
GBS forces LBS down -- to 2 at 256N and 1 at 512N -- giving back exactly the
batch lever that produced the MFU. That is the real tension: not "GBS explodes",
but "the knob that buys efficiency is the same knob a fixed GBS takes away."

Job `12473204` measures the cost directly by running LBS=1 against LBS=4 at
every node count.

### 10.3 Tokenizer: no 64k exists, and 128k wins via memory

9.4 kept "shrink the vocab" as a surviving claim. Measured now.

All agpt models inherit **gemma-7b, 256,128** -- at dim=6144 that is 3.15B
params, **11.2% of the model**. The proposal asks for "~64k custom BPE";
nothing vendored is 64k (gemma 256k, Llama-3 128k, llama-2 32k), so it would
mean training one. `agpt_30b_llama3tok` uses the 128k Llama-3 vocab (embedding
3.15B -> 1.58B, 26.5B total).

That config **had never produced a single step**, for two bugs both mine
(`4275bdb9b`): it inherited gemma's `hf_assets_path` under a 128,256 embedding
(a tokenizer that can emit ids the model cannot index), and its docstring told
callers to pass `--tokenizer.path`, which is not a flag. Every run died in
argument parsing, invisibly, because the harness never created its log file.

| | params | LBS | tps | MFU | memory |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| gemma 256k | 28.1B | 2 | 434 | 25.99% | 80.63% |
| gemma 256k (its best) | 28.1B | 3 | 466 | 27.89% | 78.50% |
| Llama-3 128k | 26.5B | 2 | 449 | 26.17% | 61.75% |
| Llama-3 128k | 26.5B | 3 | 480 | 27.98% | 67.35% |
| **Llama-3 128k** | 26.5B | **4** | **497** | **28.99%** | 75.98% |
| Llama-3 128k | 26.5B | 5 | 507 | 29.58% | 85.84% |

**At matched LBS this is a tie** (+0.09pp at LBS=3) -- MFU is FLOP-normalized,
so a smaller model gets no free bump, and the +3% tps is just less model to
push. The win is **11-19 points of freed HBM**, which buys batch steps gemma
cannot take: it is already at 78.5% at LBS=3.

**LBS=5 reaches 29.58% MFU -- the 30B matching the 2B's small-N 29.26% on a
14x larger model.** But the returns halve each step while the memory cost
grows:

| step | tps | MFU | memory |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 -> 3 | +6.9% | +1.81pp | +5.6pp |
| 3 -> 4 | +3.5% | +1.01pp | +8.6pp |
| 4 -> 5 | +2.0% | +0.59pp | +9.9pp |

**LBS=4 is the production pick**, not LBS=5. 0.59pp is not worth running at
85.84%, where checkpoint and eval allocations have to fit in what is left.
Against gemma's best that is **+6.9% tps, +1.12pp MFU** -- and unlike the
matched-LBS comparison, that part *is* genuine per-GPU efficiency.

That number is a **same-job A/B**: job `12473202` re-ran gemma at its own best
LBS=3 alongside the 128k arms, giving 465 tps / 27.87% against the separate
job's 466 / 27.89%. Cross-job drift is therefore 0.22% tps / 0.02pp MFU, so
the tokenizer result is not an artifact of comparing across allocations.

Caveat: bigger LBS worsens the batch problem. At LBS=4 the 30B hits the 2B's
measured GBS ceiling (12,288) at **256N**, and reaches 24,576 at 512N.

**And a custom ~64k -- the proposal's actual ask -- was measured and loses.**
[exp07](../proposals/30b-exp/exp07-custom-tokenizer-feasibility.md), job
`12473205`: candidate 64k and 32k BPEs trained on a 705 MB stratified sample
of `olmo-mix-1124`, measured on 176 MB of held-out text from disjoint shards.

| corpus-weighted | tokens/MB | vs gemma |
|---|---:|---:|
| custom 64k | 239,695 | **+2.5% (worse)** |
| gemma 256k | 233,944 | -- |
| **Llama-3 128k** | **225,539** | **-3.6% (best)** |

32k is worse still (+7.8%). The custom tokenizer beats gemma on 4 of 7
domains and *still* loses the weighted total, because it loses on dclm --
94.8% of the corpus. Weighting by real token share is what makes that
visible; the unweighted table looks like a close contest.

I had projected a 5-15% in-domain gain. Wrong for two reasons: 705 MB cannot
compete with tokenizers fit on hundreds of GB, and dclm is generic web text,
precisely what Llama-3's tokenizer was already fit on at far greater scale.
"Train on your own data" pays only when your data is unusual, and 94.8% of
ours is not.

**Decision: use the vendored Llama-3 128k.** The uint16 corpus halving that
only a <=65,535 vocab unlocks (15.9 TB -> ~7.9 TB, since the corpus is stored
int32 today) is genuinely forfeited by that choice, and that is the price.

Two facts from exp07 that stand regardless: the **raw text is still on disk**
(6.9 TB of `.json.gz` under `data/`, so retokenizing is a re-run and not a
reconstruction), and the existing gemma-tokenized corpus is **never touched**
-- the sampler hard-refuses to write anywhere inside the dataset root.

### 10.4 HSDP works on the 20B, fails on the 30B

Not an OOM, though I recorded it as one twice. It dies at **71.68% memory with
18 GiB free**, inside gradient clipping:

```
trainer.py:819  clip_grad_norm_ -> clip_grad.py:106
                torch.stack([norm.to(first_device) for norm in norms])
RuntimeError: level_zero backend failed with error: 40
              (UR_RESULT_ERROR_OUT_OF_RESOURCES)
```

That is device-*resource* exhaustion (events, command lists). Four arms
(job `12473199`):

| arm | verdict |
|---|---|
| **20B + HSDP** | **works** -- 12/12 steps, 488 tps, 21.72% MFU |
| 30B + HSDP, `foreach=False` | fails, identical frame |
| 30B + HSDP, `max-norm=0` | fails (my test was broken) |
| 30B + HSDP control | fails |

**Model size is the axis.** I had argued the opposite -- that since it is not
an OOM, a smaller model would fail identically. The 20B arm refutes that in one
run. **`foreach=True` is exonerated** (unfused fails the same way), and it is
not tensor count either: both models have exactly **579** parameter tensors,
differing only in size (2,623M vs 3,147M largest). The ceiling is
per-allocation.

Bisect point would be `dim=5632` (~24B), but HSDP measured 21.72% MFU against
plain FSDP's 27.89%, so [exp03](../proposals/30b-exp/exp03-1b-proxy-design.md)'s
"+2.7% MFU, prefer HSDP" does not reproduce at this scale on XPU -- the prize
may not justify the nodes.

### 10.5 Mistakes worth keeping

- **"HSDP OOM'd", written twice**, then used to argue against the correct
  hypothesis. A refuted mechanism (memory) is not evidence against an unrelated
  hypothesis (size).
- **`--training.max-norm=0` does not bypass clipping** -- `get_total_norm` runs
  unconditionally at `distributed/utils.py:651`; `max_norm` is only read by the
  scaling call at 675. That arm was a duplicate control labelled as a bypass.
- **`--tokenizer.path` is not a flag.** Cost every llama3tok run, silently,
  because the log file was never created. Creating the log FIRST is what made
  it diagnosable.
- **Run-to-run noise is <1%, not the ~6% I assumed** -- a round-2 control gave
  434 tps / 25.96% against round 1's 434 / 25.99%. The wrong assumption had
  caused a real +5.9% compile effect to be dismissed as noise.
- **`compile` defaults ON** in `agpt()`, so a "compile" arm was a no-op.
- **I called the 128k "the better config outright"** on a +3% tps reading
  before checking that MFU is FLOP-normalized. At matched batch it is a tie.
- **`git stash pop` with nothing stashed** pops an unrelated older stash --
  mine restored an 11-day-old autostash and conflicted a generated SVG.
