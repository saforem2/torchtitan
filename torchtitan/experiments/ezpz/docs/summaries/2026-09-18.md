## Headline

**Sync 84 landed, the Aurora MoE port reached hardware-proven correctness, and
the next model ladder is built — but three apparent conclusions had to be
withdrawn before any of those claims became trustworthy.**

The 148-commit upstream sync was integrated and verified across Aurora,
Sunspot, Polaris, and Perlmutter. The MoE work stayed under
`experiments/ezpz/`; `bmm_nodrop`, `aurora_sycl`, and `aurora_full_sonic` now
run correctly on XPU. The full Sonic path trains after its SYCL extensions are
prebuilt once rather than JIT-compiled concurrently by every rank. Correctness
is established; a production-scale performance advantage is not.

A new OLMo-3-tokenizer ladder now builds at **4.64B, 9.48B, and 26.20B**
parameters. The repository's existing `olmo2tok` identifiers are retained for
compatibility; the core `tokenizer.json` is byte-identical between
`allenai/OLMo-2-1124-7B` and `allenai/Olmo-3-1025-7B`. Twelve size × optimizer
LR sweeps were submitted to Aurora's
`next-eval` queue. At this summary's cutoff, the 5B AdamW sweep had finished
with `Exit_status=0`; the other eleven remained queued behind capacity. The
training arms are deliberately gated on measured LRs.

**The 80B mechanism remains unknown.** Exact-trajectory and batch controls ran
clean, and the gradient mass was localized to `lm_head.weight`, but later runs
refuted treating that skew as the cause. DP and seed remain live variables.
The old bf16-range-overflow account remains withdrawn.

> **Links:** [production status](../production/README.md) · [dispatch log](../production/dispatch-log.md) · [umbrella `bad_alloc` investigation](../guides/known-bugs/umbrella-bad-alloc-init.md)

[`6c825fdc6`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/6c825fdc6) records

Job `8808932` converted only 2/5 seats, but the failures were not one mechanism:

| seat failure | evidence |

| oneCCL KVS timeout | bootstrap took 82 seconds against a 60-second limit at 512 nodes |

All three died during initialization, not mid-training. The PALS failure killed
its seat in 23 seconds and then left it idle for the remainder of the

## 2. Failover: two bugs fixed from real production logs

### PALS child-node selection — [ezpz PR #244](https://github.com/saforem2/ezpz/pull/244)

Commit [`ebc2a79`](https://github.com/saforem2/ezpz/commit/ebc2a79) adds the
missing Aurora PALS RPC-forward pattern. PALS reports
both the parent forwarding the request and the child that stopped answering;
failover must evict the child. The fix was verified through
`scrape_bad_nodes()` against the actual `8808932` log:

- extracts the unreachable child;
- rejects the healthy reporting parent;
- returns no bad node for clean logs;
- 39 tests pass.

### `std::bad_alloc` classification — [ezpz PR #245](https://github.com/saforem2/ezpz/pull/245) / [issue #243](https://github.com/saforem2/ezpz/issues/243)

Commits [`f229667`](https://github.com/saforem2/ezpz/commit/f229667) and
[`190c1ad`](https://github.com/saforem2/ezpz/commit/190c1ad) add the narrow
`std::bad_alloc` signature to the Python and shell crash classifiers. Without it, `rc=143` fell through to
`walltime`, disabling useful recovery. The error had occurred five times.

The broad `MemoryError` pattern was deliberately rejected: the same regex set
is consumed on an `rc == 0` path, where a benign “recovered from MemoryError”
line would turn a successful run into a retry. The narrow C++ literal fixes the
real log without that false positive. This fixes classification, not the
allocator failure itself; with no scrapeable host, recovery still begins with
one blind rotation.

## 4. MoE port: correctness proven, performance still open

> **Links:** [four-machine backend report](../experiments/moe-expert-backends-4machine.md) · [Aurora MoE training guide](../guides/aurora-moe-training.md) · [PR #17](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/pull/17)

The contribution boundary held: [PR #17](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/pull/17)
moved 108 files under `experiments/ezpz/`
([`62c9a576a`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/62c9a576a)), and the
native-DDP configuration was added
through `EzpzParallelismConfig` without changing core configuration classes.
The existing `EzpzGroupedExperts` subclass became the home for Sam Wheeler's

`models/common/moe.py`.

Key backend results:

- `bmm_nodrop`
  ([`831e0b24c`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/831e0b24c)) is
  bit-exact against the bf16 reference, including an expert receiving zero
  tokens. It differs intentionally from the existing capacity-factor BMM: it
  pads to the observed maximum and drops no tokens.
- `aurora_sycl`
  ([`987177d73`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/987177d73),
  [`ce6570508`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/ce6570508)) agrees
  within approximately `6.104e-05`. Its earlier failure was a compiler/runtime stack mismatch, not a
  kernel-correctness result. Job `8829416` independently verified it on XPU.
- EP geometry was checked at EP=2 and across EP=2/8/12 (`8836014`, `8836277`).
- `aurora_full_sonic` initially segfaulted in backward because 24 ranks tried to
  JIT-compile the same extensions concurrently. Prebuild job `8837246`
  established a shared cache; smoke `8837281` then trained cleanly:
  `12.95230 → 12.59623 → 11.47120`, `rc=0`, zero non-finite values, and about
  `5e-05` agreement with the EP=2 reference.

The last point is the durable deployment rule: **prebuild the SYCL extensions
once, then launch ranks against the shared cache.**

Performance is unresolved. Aurora 10B comparisons `8837353` and `8837465`
were under-budgeted from debug-model timing and reached zero steps before being
stopped. The smaller Sunspot comparison `12478094` was still in flight at the

## 5. The 80B: localization is not a mechanism

> **Links:** [DP design review](../experiments/80b-dp-design-review-2026-09-08.md) · [80B production notes](../production/agpt/80b/README.md)

Exact-trajectory and matched-GBS controls completed cleanly: 25/25 and 40/40
steps with no non-finite values. At healthy steps, `lm_head.weight` carried a
gradient norm of 0.2520 against approximately 0.005693 for the next-largest
tensor — about **44× concentration**. That moves the dominant gradient outside
the transformer blocks and weakens attention-only remedies as complete
explanations.

A five-arm DP study then completed **177 clean steps**. `lm_head` was relatively
DP-invariant while layer gradients averaged down, increasing the skew with DP.
But the proposed explanation did not survive the trajectory:

- excursions were reversible;
- another excursion occurred at constant LR;
- skew kept increasing while loss recovered;
- [`602bb8fbb`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/602bb8fbb)
  records skew tripling as loss fell.

The conclusion is narrower than the investigation: the historical 80B failure
is not a deterministic function of LR trajectory, global batch size, optimizer,
and step count alone. DP and seed remain live. Preserve the tensor-named,
pre-clip diagnostics, but do not call gradient skew causal.

One record correction matters: control job `12473149` completed 120/120 steps,
loss `12.95 → 8.098`, with zero NaNs. The older bf16 range-overflow account is
still retracted: bf16 and fp32 share exponent width, and historical job
`8574385` failed at effective LR `3.87e-9`, far below the nominal ceiling.

## 6. The OLMo-3 tokenizer ladder

> **Links:** [ladder plan](../experiments/optimizer-comparison/2026-09-18-olmo2tok-ladder-plan.md) · [30B optimizer comparison](../experiments/optimizer-comparison/README.md) · [OLMo-3 model](https://huggingface.co/allenai/Olmo-3-1025-7B)

The prior 30B OLMo-3-tokenizer campaign established feasibility on Sunspot:
2,000/2,000 steps, loss `12.028 → 2.115`, zero NaN/inf, and 28.3% MFU. At step
2,000, HellaSwag was 27.14% (`+4.8σ`) and MMLU 26.24% (`+3.3σ`), combined
Stouffer `+4.81σ`. This was a proposal-validation campaign, not Aurora
production.

The new ladder uses the 100,352-token OLMo-3 vocabulary. The 5B and 10B
configurations landed in
[`0065c0304`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/0065c0304), their
pre-cached Grain data path in
[`e65543639`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/e65543639), and the
ladder documentation/index in
[`1cb05b100`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/1cb05b100):

| flavor | dim | layers | heads | parameters |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `5B_olmo2tok` | 3,072 | 40 | 24 | **4.64B** |
| `10B_olmo2tok` | 4,096 | 48 | 32 | **9.48B** |
| `30B_olmo2tok` | 6,144 | 64 | 48 | **26.20B** |

The tokenizer is staged (7.1 MB), and the olmo-mix wiki subset is precached
(6.1 GB, two shards). Grain tokenizes raw text per sample with the configured
tokenizer, so the 6.97 TB full corpus does not need an eager retokenization.
The dataset builder had initially been set to `parquet`; olmo-mix ships
`.json.gz`, and
[`ff4f7aa4b`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/ff4f7aa4b)
corrected it to `json`.

No training arm may inherit the shared `lr=8e-4`: the 20B production run uses
`2.28e-5`, and the correct value is size- and optimizer-specific. The launcher
was corrected in
[`2e46c9cdf`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/2e46c9cdf) so it
cannot silently force `blendcorpus` and feed Gemma token IDs into a
100,352-entry OLMo-3 embedding. The `next-eval` calibration path then landed in
[`57713af8f`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/57713af8f), with
external-venv and required-symbol preflight fixes in
[`cd041e4cd`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/cd041e4cd) and
[`71801d01d`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/71801d01d).

## 7. Twelve LR sweeps, and two tooling bugs caught before launch

> **Links:** [sweep report and job map](../experiments/lr-finder/agpt/2026-09-18-olmo2tok-ladder-gbs6144-nexteval.md) · [LR-finder index](../experiments/lr-finder/agpt/README.md)

The final Aurora `next-eval` sweep map is 3 sizes × 4 optimizers, 64 nodes and
6 hours per job, GBS 6144:

| optimizer | 4.64B | 9.48B | 26.20B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| AdamW | `8837334` | `8837335` | `8837336` |
| Mano | `8837373` | `8837375` | `8837377` |
| SophiaG | `8837391` | `8837392` | `8837393` |
| Muon | `8837397` | `8837398` | `8837399` |

Muon gets a wider sweep
([`27f9452f2`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/27f9452f2)).
The known 30B/GBS=960 blow-up is `5.68e-3`, 5.7×
above the original `1e-3` ceiling; the first Muon jobs would have exited 0 with
no recommendation after consuming 18 node-hours. Stale jobs `8837374`,
`8837376`, and `8837378` were deleted before running and replaced by the jobs
above.

Two silent-analysis failures were caught:

1. A monitor glob matched every optimizer's output directory, so one
   optimizer's newest log could be reported under another job's name. Monitoring
   is now scoped per job and optimizer.
2. The first summarizer
   ([`852f7ba76`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/852f7ba76),
   corrected in
   [`fb062f51a`](https://github.com/saforem2/torchtitan/commit/fb062f51a))
   selected `cands[0]` from `find_optimal_lr`, but that
   function returns every derivative crossing. On real historical AdamW data,
   the first crossing was a noise wiggle at `2.31e-6`; the actual cliff was
   `3.95e-3` near the loss minimum — a **~1,700× error** that still looked like a
   valid number. The summarizer now chooses the crossing at or after the loss
   minimum and reports the candidate count.

Synthetic fixtures did not catch the second bug because each was constructed
with exactly one minimum. Real CSVs did, and the fixed result reproduced on
both the workstation and Aurora.

At the summary cutoff, `8837334` (5B/AdamW) had completed with exit 0. The other
11 jobs remained queued with scheduler comments indicating capacity or
reservation pressure, not job failure. Artifact analysis still gates the
training arms; exit 0 alone is not a result.

## 8. Optimizer evidence carried into the ladder

> **Links:** [optimizer-comparison write-up](../experiments/optimizer-comparison/README.md) · [W&B report](https://api.wandb.ai/links/aurora_gpt/0em3ktti) · [W&B project](https://wandb.ai/aurora_gpt/agpt-30b-optcmp)

The completed 30B fixed-batch campaign remains the prior:

- Mano led AdamW by 0.143 nats at 10B tokens and 0.075 at 23.59B.
- AdamW closed at approximately 0.0187 nats per 1,000 steps, suggesting a
  possible crossover around 35–40B tokens.
- The campaign used one seed per arm and no decay phase.
- SophiaG diverged 4/4 and must not be launched without a gradient-norm abort.
- Muon has an LR measurement but no completed 30B training arm.

Prior 30B/GBS=960 LR-finder cliffs were AdamW `3.05e-4`, SophiaG `3.55e-4`,
Mano `5.61e-4`, and Muon `5.68e-3`. They set sweep windows, not the new ladder's
training LRs; those must come from the matching size and GBS.
