## The short version

This was a productive stretch, although not a tidy one.

PR #21 finally landed after exact-head tests on Aurora, Sunspot, Polaris, and
Perlmutter. More importantly, the Aurora tests used the runtime we actually
care about: the `next-eval` queue's oneAPI 2026.1.0 stack with our isolated
Torch 2.15 environment, rather than the broken PyTorch 2.13 environment bundled
with `frameworks/2026.1.0`.

The 5B and 10B LR work also produced several usable numbers. We have solid
AdamW recommendations for both sizes and a solid 5B SophiaG result. The gaps
are now narrow and explicit: 10B SophiaG still needs a lower-range measurement,
and neither 30B optimizer has a complete fine curve.

On production, one 2B chain finished, both 20B chains made real progress, and
one 2B constant-LR chain turned out to have been non-finite for much longer
than the latest umbrella suggested. We traced that trajectory back to a sharp
boundary between steps 39,942 and 39,943. The latest checkpoints are not viable
resume points; an exact replay from step 39,900 is queued to catch the first
bad tensor.

Finally, the scary 768-rank Aurora `std::bad_alloc` is no longer an amorphous
"30B OOM." A clean communicator ladder passed at 48, 192, 384, and twice at
768 ranks. That does not prove the 30B model fits through its first optimizer
update, but it does clear the XCCL topology itself under the current runtime.

## PR #21: landed, with the actual runtime

The runtime distinction matters enough to state plainly. Aurora's `next-eval`
image already supplies oneAPI 2026.1.0. Setting `LRF_SKIP_MODULE_LOAD=1` skips
the `frameworks/2026.1.0` conda environment—and therefore its broken PyTorch
2.13—not oneAPI itself. The tested stack is **oneAPI 2026.1.0 plus our isolated
Torch 2.15 environment**.

PR #21 head `1f4aff12b` passed on four systems:

The route there was noisier than the final table implies. Several earlier

## Aurora XCCL: from a vague OOM to a closed communicator gate

Job `8862818` was initially summarized as an OOM. The detailed trace says
something more specific: it failed before model training, when the first seed
broadcast lazily created XCCL communicators. Many ranks threw
`ProcessGroupXCCL::initXCCLComm` / `std::bad_alloc`; PMI/KVS timeouts followed
as fallout. No optimizer step ran.

We replaced that ambiguous result with a communicator-only ladder using the
same oneAPI 2026.1.0 + Torch 2.15 archive and the same `dp_shard=8` geometry:

| job | active nodes | ranks | mesh | result |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `8870482` | 4 | 48 | `6×8` | pass |
| `8870561` | 16 | 192 | `24×8` | pass |
| `8870915` | 32 | 384 | `48×8` | pass |
| `8870976` | 64 | 768 | `96×8` | pass |
| `8871023` | 64 | 768 | `96×8` | independent repeat, pass |

Every row has `XCCL_MESH_PROBE_PASS`, a job-specific `VALIDATED` artifact, and
PBS exit 0. The two 768-rank runs used the same immutable source and runtime and
showed none of the earlier `std::bad_alloc`, communicator, PMI/KVS, traceback,
or rank-death signatures.

That closes the communicator gate. It does **not** magically finish the 30B LR
campaign; the next 30B test must still construct the model and complete real
optimizer updates.

## Production: real progress, plus one poisoned trajectory

Umbrella `8855988` ran for 6h45m before an external PBS exit `-14`. The seats
did not all have the same outcome:

- **2B-512 stage 2** reached its planned endpoint at step 23,746, saved the final checkpoint, and exited cleanly. Its original red dashboard badge was our parser mistaking `FAILOVER STOP: success` for a failure.
- **20B-512 constant LR** remained numerically healthy through step 11,191, then lost a shepherd to `SIGKILL`. Step 11,100 is a complete checkpoint.
- **20B-256** reached step 16,899; step 16,800 is complete.
- **2B-256 stage 2** reached step 35,300.
- **2B-512 constant LR** stayed non-finite throughout the umbrella run.

The last case is now localized. The original trajectory was healthy through
step 39,942—loss `2.69423`, grad norm `0.3955`—and became NaN at step 39,943.
The umbrella later restored step 42,400, which was already downstream of that
event, so its immediate NaN was not a new resume regression. It was continuing
a poisoned model.

Diagnostic job `8870516` is queued to replay the exact production topology from
step 39,900 through the boundary. It restores full model, optimizer, scheduler,
dataloader, and RNG state; records per-step and per-layer diagnostics; aborts
on the first non-finite result; and writes only to an isolated output tree.

A successor production umbrella, `8870515`, is also queued. It carries only the
three healthy unfinished seats: 20B-512, 20B-256, and 2B-256 stage 2. The
completed 2B-512 stage-2 seat and poisoned constant-LR seat are excluded. It
uses a 24-hour, 2,098-node `large` allocation because Aurora's `medium` class
caps walltime at 18 hours and `large` begins at 2,000 nodes.

## Evaluations: what is actually covered

The MDS stage-3 endpoints at step 154,391 were evaluated. At 7.771T tokens:

| endpoint | MMLU | what stood out |
|---|---:|---|
| stage-3 mix | `0.2463` | ARC-Easy `0.7138`, ARC-Challenge@25 `0.4164` |
| stage-3 math/code | `0.2591` | substantial commonsense forgetting |

The result is fairly stark: more tokens and a different finishing mix did not
move MMLU off chance. The broader stage-3 mix produced our strongest
commonsense numbers, while the narrow math/code finish gave some of that back.

The more recent post-training sequence is also complete:

- Stage-1 checkpoints 300/600/900 each produced 200 GSM8K generations: 47, 52, and 48 exact answers respectively. The eventual exit-143 records were teardown cleanup after the artifacts were complete.
- Stage-2 SFT `12478614` completed 93/93 updates.
- Stage-2 eval `12478618` produced 200 generations: 197 were format-valid and 43 were strictly correct.
- GRPO `12478621` completed 20/20 real updates with checkpoints at 10 and 20.

We have **not** evaluated every stored 2B and 20B checkpoint, nor should we.
There are hundreds of 100-step snapshots. The completed backfills cover selected
ladders and endpoints, but the 20B tails have advanced beyond the documented
evaluation coverage. The next useful backfill is a sparse, token-aware sample of
those new tails—not a brute-force pass over every checkpoint.
