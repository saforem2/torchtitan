investigation that found it.** `agpt_80b()` is a one-liner inheriting
`agpt()`'s shared `lr=8e-4` -- fine at 2B, and **588x past the documented NaN
onset (1.36e-6) and 1081x past the usable ceiling (~7.4e-7)** at 80B. Job
`12474431` at 5e-7 on the same 64 nodes, same dp=192: 12 steps, **0 non-finite

## 1. The 80B: one real finding, four retractions

Read in order, because the order is the content.

**What survived.** Overflow is refuted by measurement.
`diag/qk_q_absmax_local` reads **61.2** at 48, 72 and 84 layers at dp=192, and
62.25 at dp=12 in the clean regime -- **36 orders of magnitude** below the bf16
ceiling of 3.39e38, with `grad_absmax` peaking at 0.03. These are the first
activation magnitudes ever recorded in the failing configuration; the repo
previously held no amax artifact at all. This one stands because activation
scale does not depend on the LR being right.

**What did not survive.**

| claim | how it died |
|---|---|
| "L84 died at step 40; two back-to-back events are fatal" | No arm died. L84 went non-finite at 40 and 59 and recovered both times -- step 41 reads loss 6.98344 / grad_norm 7.4190. Retracted 5 minutes after the sharper mechanism was built on it. |
| "Event rate scales with depth, 0/1/8 across 48/72/84 layers" | Those were single runs. The replicate in the identical configuration came back **2 events against L84's 8** -- a 4x spread from data order alone. |
| "GBS, not dp" | Void twice: once for the LR, and separately because G8 and G32 are indistinguishable where the proposed exposure mechanism predicts G32 should be ~4x worse. |
| the accumulation-exposure analysis | built on the above |

The stated pattern behind all three retractions: proposing a mechanism before
verifying the conditions the data was collected under.

**The GAS sweep's own verdict was the wrong measurement.** At fixed dp=192, G1
(192 seqs) ran 60 steps with 2 recoverable events; G8 (1,536 seqs) and G32
(6,144 seqs, production GBS) both start at loss 12.95, jump to 43.05 and 46.20,
and go non-finite at step 2 -- the first optimizer step. The report printed
"larger batches are MORE stable per unit work" off G32's per-microbatch rate of
0.0156. G32 ran two steps and blew up on the second: it did not survive, it ran
out of steps. A rate normaliser assumes scattered events and cannot express a
deterministic blow-up at the first update.

Worth noting how that slipped through: the verdict logic passed 5/5 synthetic
cases covering flat, rising, falling, null and missing-arm rate patterns. All
five tested whether the rate was **computed** correctly. None tested whether a
rate was the right metric.

**A hardware constraint, recorded rather than re-hit.** Holding GBS fixed while
removing gradient accumulation needs larger per-rank microbatches, and both
32,768 and 16,384 tokens/rank SIGTERM in the first forward. The XPU-side
allocation failure kills the process instead of raising, so there is no OOM
text to grep -- the tell is `rc=143` twenty-six seconds after "Training starts
at step 1". The no-accumulation control cannot be built on this hardware.

**A probe that has never fired.** The QK amax probe's `observe()` returns
immediately under `torch.compiler.is_compiling()` because it calls `.item()`.
It has a caller (`agpt/__init__.py:141`) but has never produced a value in a
compiled production run.

## 2. SophiaG: delay, not prevention

Declared resolved (`d2f735498`), retracted the same day (`f085e079f`), and the
correction propagated (`93c850378`). Halving the LR moves the divergence; the

run.

## 4. Production

Two umbrellas, and the second is the best result the pattern has produced.

| seat | `8784460` | `8784462` |
|---|---|---|
| `t0` 2b-n512 | no steps | no steps |
| `t1` 20b-n512 | no steps | **9,562** / 2.40145 |
| `t2` 20b-n256 | died at 7h27m | **13,136** / 2.39209 |
| `t3` 2b-n512 | 24,800 / 2.72772 | **27,917** / 2.71773 |
| `t4` 2b-n256 | 4,255 / 2.51342 | **8,400** / 2.49624 |

20B-256 advanced **12,400 -> 13,100** on disk. `supervise_trainer` earned its
place: `t2` trained 7h27m in the first job, died, and was relaunched three
times before the quick-death cap stopped it -- without it, 266 nodes would have
sat idle for the last 4.5 hours.

**`t0` narrowed.** It failed in three consecutive umbrellas. Four hypotheses
eliminated:

- **not bad hardware** -- the two failing runs shared **zero** hosts
- **not a 512N/6144-rank ceiling** -- `t3` is the identical shape and trained
  to 27,917 in the same job
- **not a `t0`+`t1` pair** -- `t1` trained fine in `8784462`; I had recorded
  that pairing as reproducible and it was not
- **not oneCCL PMI** -- `pmrt_kvs_get` is downstream; the terminal error is
  `MemoryError: std::bad_alloc`, matching `8463182` and `8466848` from May

What remains: `t0` is the stage-2 dolmino seat, resuming a different checkpoint
shape with a different step target. That is the next thing to test.

**ezpz issue #243 filed.** `rc=143` is classified as `walltime`, so failover
stops with 10 spares unused and ~11h55m of allocation left. Reproduces across
4 seats in 2 jobs. 143 is `128 + 15` -- PBS walltime produces it, but so does
the umbrella's supervisor and the internal watchdog, so the code alone collapses
several causes into the one classification that disables recovery. Second time
this banner has cost an allocation; the first was the `step=` vs `step:` regex.
