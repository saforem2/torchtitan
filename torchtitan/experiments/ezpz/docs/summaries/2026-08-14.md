## 2. Sunspot: reducing collectives were broken; the RC fixes them

Two days of 80B work turned out to be chasing one fault.

**Symptom.** `agpt_80b` at 4N/TP=4 -- a config that ran 10/10 clean on 08-03
(job `12472452`) -- SIGSEGV'd on every rank inside step 1, in
`_redistribute_outputs` -> `reduce_scatter_tensor`.

**It was not our code.** An A/B at the 08-03 commit and at HEAD failed
*identically* (job `12473110`, 60 segv each, 0 steps). A ~40-line torch-only
script -- no torchtitan, no DTensor, no model -- segfaults on a **1 KiB buffer
with 12 ranks on a single node**, so no fabric is involved either. Reproduced in
6 allocations with a different failing rank each time (34, 12, 33, 18, 8, 4), so
not a bad node.

**It is the reduction specifically.** Same node, same ranks, same buffers:

| collective | result |
|---|---|
| `all_gather_into_tensor` | **passes** all sizes to 144 MiB/rank |
| `reduce_scatter_tensor` | SIGSEGV |
| `all_reduce` | SIGSEGV |

Both failures reduce on device; the one that passes only moves bytes. Since
`all_reduce` is DDP's and FSDP's gradient reduction, this blocked **all**
multi-rank training, not just TP>1.

**The frameworks RC fixes it.** On `frameworks/2026.1.0` (oneCCL `26.181.0` +
torch `2.13.0a0+gitcf30153`) all three collectives run clean. No workaround
exists among ten standard CCL knobs (transport, IPC exchange, immediate
commandlists, monolithic kernel, worker count).

Docs: [sunspot-reduce-scatter-segv-20260814](../guides/known-bugs/sunspot-reduce-scatter-segv-20260814.md)
(includes a verified one-node repro + a drafted ALCF ticket) and
[`tests/repro_reduce_scatter_segv.py`](../../tests/repro_reduce_scatter_segv.py).

## 4. The RC does NOT fix the 80B NaN -- and fp32 shows why

bf16 at dp=192 on the RC **NaN'd at step 30** (job `12473142`), landing in the
historical 14/17/19/38 ladder. **Wall 1 is stack-independent.**

Running the same config with fp32 activations (job `12473149`) exposed the
mechanism:

| step | bf16 grad_norm | fp32 grad_norm | bf16 loss | fp32 loss |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 8.33 | **17,418** | 15.46 | 12.84 |
| 7 | 7.86 | **91,467** | 12.90 | 12.75 |
| 11 | 8.05 | **80,065** | 12.76 | **12.39** |

**bf16 is not mis-reporting a large gradient -- it computes a different, smaller
one.** bf16 has fp32's full exponent range (max ~3.4e38), so 18,000 is trivially
representable; 65504 is the *fp16* limit. What bf16 loses is its **7-bit
mantissa**, destroying the small contributions whose accumulation across 84
residual layers produces those spikes. Mantissa, not exponent -- exactly what
the root-cause report said.

Corollaries:

- `grad_norm ~= 8` in every historical 80B bf16 log is a real value for a
  *degraded* gradient, not a masked reading of the true one.
- **Clipping is not broken.** It scales by `1/total_norm` using the norm computed
  in that run, and the bf16 run genuinely has norm ~8.
- **fp32 learns faster** (12.39 vs 12.76 at step 11, accelerating), which a pure
  reporting artifact could not produce.

Measured cost at dp=192: memory 36.5 GiB (57%) vs 20.5 (32%); **16 tps vs 54 =
3.4x slower**, matching the documented 3-5x.

At the time of writing the fp32 run is at step 23, clean, past the 14/17/19
points. Step 30 -- where bf16 died at this dp -- is the open question.

### 9.5 The embedding was frozen too -- and nobody had checked

[exp02](../production/agpt/30b-exp/exp02-fp32-norms-ablation.md), three arms,
identical seed/data/steps, differing only in the master copy (job `8757151`):

| arm | norms frozen | `tok_embeddings` frac_changed |
|---|---|---|
| A bf16 | **13/13** | 0.000346 |
| B **norms-only fp32** | 0/13 | **0.000345** |
| C full fp32 | 0/13 | **1.000000** |

**38.36% of all parameter elements never move under norms-only.** Cause: agpt
initialises the embedding at `std=1.0` (`_EMBEDDING_INIT`) -- the *same scale
as `RMSNorm.weight`*, hence the same ~7.8e-3 bf16 ULP. The v1 post-mortem
reasoned only about linear layers at std~0.005 and never checked the embedding.
**The bug had already recurred in the shipped 2B/20B/80B configs.**

Coverage ruled out decisively (job `8757243`): restricted to the 91/32,000 rows
that actually received gradient -- identical rows in all arms -- full fp32
moves 1.0000 while norms-only moves 0.1471, indistinguishable from the broken
arm. QK-norm confirmed too: bf16 freezes **25/25** norms with it enabled.

And full fp32 is **also the fastest** (54.0 vs 39.0 vs 29.8 tps at 24 ranks) --
FSDP2 forbids mixed dtypes per shard group, so norms-only needs its own
collectives. It loses on correctness, throughput, and complexity at once.

**Loss cannot see any of this**: all three arms within 0.005 nats, and the
*broken* arm has the lowest final loss.

### 9.6 fp32 master does NOT force fp32 inference

[exp04](../production/agpt/30b-exp/exp04-fp32-inference-investigation.md),
answering a deployment objection. Training compute is bf16 either way, and
**the exported safetensors are uniformly BF16 (111/111 tensors)** -- the fp32
master never reaches the served checkpoint. The completed 2B is byte-identical
bf16-vs-fp32 over a 200-token greedy decode.

It also **refutes the recorded cause** of the vLLM garbage output (four docs
blame vocab 256000 + ffn 11008 for bf16 error); that exact architecture
generates coherently in bf16 through HF. Real cause **not isolated** -- by
elimination it is inside vLLM, but ckpt-900's safetensors are deleted and vLLM
will not init on a login node, so this is inference, not measurement.

Three unrelated bugs found: no `torch_dtype` in any of 40 exported
`config.json`; bos/eos swapped; and a **live RoPE-flavor trap** --
`eval-2b-v2.sh:69` defaults to the complex flavor while the newer clones train
`_real`. The completed 2B chains are *safe* (their clone predates the `_real`
default, so they trained complex and were converted correctly, corroborated by
HellaSwag rising monotonically 0.405 -> 0.561), but every newer chain is
exposed. Task #73.
