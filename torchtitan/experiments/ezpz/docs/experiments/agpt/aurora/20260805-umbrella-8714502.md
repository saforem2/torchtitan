## Why it ended

Two unrelated causes, neither numerical:

**Trainer 0 (04:27)** -- clean `FAILOVER STOP: walltime (rc=143)` after 17,971s.
Hit its own budget, saved, exited. Working as designed.

**Trainer 1 (05:50)** -- Aurora infrastructure:

```
ping failed on x4713c6s2b0n0: No reply from x4410c7s1b0n0 after 97s
[auto-retry] blind rotation: x4713c5s2b0n0...
launch failed: Couldn't forward RPC launch(...) : Resource temporarily unavailable
[auto-retry] FAILOVER STOP: stuck_pre_training (rc=127)
```

A node went unresponsive, auto-retry correctly rotated it out, and the
**relaunch** hit the known Aurora pals RPC fault. Failover gave up. The umbrella
then SIGTERM'd trainers 2/3/4 at the same second (`05:50:21`), which is why
trainer 2 stops mid-stride at step 7897 with nothing wrong with it.

So the tally is: **1 clean walltime exit, 1 infra fault, 3 collateral kills** --
not five failures.

## Three things worth following up

**1. 20B-512 loss ROSE across the run: 2.18 -> 2.41.** Not noise, and not a
spike -- it climbs monotonically over ~350 steps:

```
step 6801  2.2336      step 7009  2.4069
step 6871  2.2771      step 7113  2.4227
step 6905  2.3651      step 7148  2.4150
```

The chain resumed from step-6800 and immediately drifted upward. Both 20B chains
run `decay-ratio=0.0` (constant LR 2.28e-5), so this is not a schedule artifact.
Compare trainer 2 on the same config, which stayed flat (2.3765 -> 2.3710) over
400 steps. **A resume that comes back 0.23 nats worse and keeps climbing is worth
explaining before the next 512N dispatch** -- candidates: optimizer state not
restored cleanly across the resume, a data-order/shard difference, or genuine
instability at this LR for the 512N batch. Note the 256N chain now reports a
LOWER loss (2.371) than the 512N chain (2.415) despite having seen roughly half
the tokens.

**2. 2B-512 MFU is ~3-9%, against 17-20% for the 20B trainers.** Ramps 0.46% ->
~8% and oscillates 5-9% for the rest of the run, ending at 2.70%. tflops 8.04
versus 52-59 for the 20B chains. That is a large amount of wasted 512-node
allocation and has been true for a while (this is the same chain that has been
"queue-starved" in the dashboard). Worth a separate look at whether it is
comms-bound at this size.

**3. Trainers 3 and 4 never trained a step** yet both report
`FAILOVER STOP: walltime (rc=143, attempt 1)` -- after 618s and 1109s
respectively. A "walltime" stop 10 minutes into a 24h job is a mislabel; they
died during setup and the failover taxonomy assigned the wrong reason. Trainer 3
was a duplicate 2B-512 slot (the same chain as trainer 0, so it would have
collided anyway); trainer 4 was 2B-256, which is already COMPLETE at 4.674T, so
neither loss matters -- but **the mislabel does**, because it hides setup
failures behind a benign-looking reason.
